I like coding with agents: it broadens my horizons and skills. I've got a ton of ideas, but the problem is that many of them either require writing huge amounts of code or involve areas I know nothing about—areas that demand an insane amount of boilerplate or knowledge of "under-the-hood" magic. Like writing a small, native macOS GUI app to display an icon and specific data in the menu bar.

Or take yesterday: I wrote a clone of the Windows 2000 "Starfield Simulation" screensaver for Mac using just a single prompt. Link: https://github.com/ilirium/starfield_simulation_screensaver_win2k_for_macos

And today I wrote the screensaver uninstaller—a task that took far longer than expected, simply because macOS doesn't provide proper built-in tools for it (in 2026!).

For instance, the screensaver preview gets stored in the system cache, and that's what shows up in System Settings. But there's no clear way to refresh the cache—you just have to delete it manually, the damn thing.

Still, the AI handled it all under my expert guidance :)

I have an analogy: AI models are taking us back to the golden age of the late 80s and early 90s, when there was little boilerplate, practically only one programming language, and it was much easier to launch a solo project in any field.

I didn't experience that era firsthand, but I've read a lot about it. People who claim that coding with AI isn't a creative act likely don't understand how things used to be—or they're simply opposed to any change to the status quo.

Meanwhile, those who have been programming since the 80s or earlier have lived through several revolutions; they know the field is constantly shifting, knowledge becomes obsolete, and you have to keep adapting.
